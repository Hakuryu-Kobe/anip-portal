INTR8060 · China's Global Engagement · Research essay

Instrumental Effect of Century of Humiliation in Foreign Policy

Focusing on Sino-Japanese Diplomacy

Foreign Policy AnalysisChina–Japan RelationsHistorical MemoryQualitative Comparison
Research question

To what extent does the “Century of Humiliation” shape China's foreign policy, compared with contemporary security and economic interests?

Overview

This essay examines the instrumental role of historical memory in China's diplomacy toward Japan. Drawing on Chinese Ministry of Foreign Affairs press-conference statements, it compares two Taiwan-related disputes (2024 and 2025) with a 2025 exchange on regional economic cooperation. Historical references, particularly Japan's colonial responsibility, can reinforce China's contemporary sovereignty claims, whereas economic cooperation rhetoric foregrounds mutual benefit. The comparison suggests that the visibility of historical memory in official discourse varies across issues and can complement rather than simply compete with strategic interests. Because the evidence is limited to public statements, the essay does not establish whether historical memory independently determines policy decisions or shapes underlying national interests.

Approach & skills
  • Comparative qualitative analysis of three official diplomatic exchanges
  • Operational distinction between instrumental and constitutive effects of historical memory
  • Source criticism and explicit consideration of question-framing and causal-inference limitations
INTR8046 · International Relations · Research essay

Borders as a Core Principle of International System

Borders from Kellogg-Briand Pact to UN Charter and Further

International LawTerritorial IntegrityInternational OrderHistorical Analysis
Research question

What is the place of borders in the evolution of the international system?

Overview

This essay traces the emergence of legal protection for territorial borders as a central principle of the modern international system. Focusing on the period from the 1928 Kellogg–Briand Pact to the 1945 United Nations Charter, it examines the prohibition of war, the Stimson Doctrine's non-recognition principle, and the Charter's restrictions on the threat or use of force. The analysis also considers the 1970 Friendly Relations Declaration and the gap between legal norms and state practice. It argues that international rules shifted from permitting territorial acquisition through conquest toward protecting territorial integrity. This transformation was neither linear nor inevitable, but arose through legal and political responses to distinct historical circumstances.

Approach & skills
  • Historical and legal analysis of treaties, doctrines, and UN instruments
  • Synthesis of international relations and international law scholarship
  • Distinction between legal principles, norm development, and state compliance
